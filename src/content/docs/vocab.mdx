## Vocabulary

There is a lot of theory on communicating with our furry friends. But this is not the place! [(More information on communication theory here!)](/training/theory)

<Image src={abcs} alt="ABCs" />

### Cue

A cue is an invitation for your dog to execute a behavior.

### Behavior

A behavior is a broad term to apply to any observable action. To make a square/rectangle analogy; a trick is a behavior but a behavior can also be any number of things.

### Marker

A marker always comes after a behavior and helps shaping the current behavior to a new behavior. A positive marker should almost always be paired with a treat.

## Markers

Markers are similar to verbal cues, but they are used to note specific things to the dog. They are a part of the reward structure and so separate from cues and don't really need to be trained in the same way.

One could also use a clicker instead-of or in addition to verbal markers. I personally prefer verbal markers as it is one less physical device needed.

These are markers that I use when working with dogs. IMO, they don't really need to be trained as their association comes over time and consistent usage.

<Cue
  title="Yes!"
  subtitle="Postitive Terminal"
>
"You did the thing, and you're done!"

##### When it is useful

This marker is the default positive affirmation that I use when training. It signals "yes, you did it!" It is short, sweet, and too the point.

It is notably destinct from "Good" in that it signals they have fully executed either a single behavior or sequence of behaviors.

##### Important note

"Yes!" only works as the postive marker because it is associated with the value of the treat.

If you do not give treats with the marker, or give other markers with treats, the value of the marker degrades over time. So consitency really is key.

:::note[Resources]

- [Practical getting started guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URY5UqUYoSY)

  Happy Hounds Training provides a great resource to getting started with reinforcing positive marker training.

:::

</Cue>

<Cue
  title="Good"
  subtitle="Positive Continuation"
>
"You're doing the thing, keep going!"

##### When it is useful

Anytime you are training a sustained behavior like "stay", "sit", or "down."

"Good" can also be useful when you are trying to execute a sequence of tricks and want to maintain the dogs attention.

##### Important note

"Good" similarly to "Yes" only works as the postive marker because it is associated with the value of the treat.

"Good" is a bit more nuanced then "Yes" in terms of when to provide treats in the reward structure. For example if trying to execute "roll over" and the dog has not learned it the verbal command yet. You may need to execute "down" first, and then you may say "good" and immediately begin luring the dog into roll over. The important thing in such a case is to make sure to praise or "Yes!" after the end of the execution.

</Cue>

<Cue
  title="No"
  subtitle="Correction"
>

"Sorry, that is not the desired behavior"

##### IMPORTANT NOTE

"No" is not a punishment!!!

"No" simply notes that the behavior the dog is currently exibiting is not the desired behavior.

Keeping the phrase to 1 syllable is the most polite thing you can do for your dog, because it is the clearest to them.
"Sorry, that is not the desired behavior", "Please don't do that" are what we mean, but it is asking a lot for your dog to digest all of that.

## Core Cues

These are the core cues taught by the [Good Pup](https://goodpupip-petcarenow.talentlms.com/plus/) training program as essential for building a basis of communication with your dog.

I use thse cues as building blocks for [games](/training/games) and [more fun stuff](#advanced).

<Cue
  title="Come"
  blurb="Dog immediately returns to the handler regardless of environment or distractions."
  usefulFor="Critical safety recall, off-leash play, calling dog in from yard or dog park."
  howToTrain="Start in low-distraction environment with high-value rewards, call with enthusiastic tone, mark and reward heavily on arrival."
  nonVerbal="2 mouth clicks (or opening arms wide)."
/>

<Cue
  title="Sit"
  blurb="4 paws + butt on the ground. You know it, you love it. Probably every dogs first “trick.”"
  usefulFor="Always. Ideally "
  howToTrain="Hold a treat close to the dog's nose, slowly move it up and back over their head until their bottom hits the floor, mark ('Yes!'), and reward."
  nonVerbal="Open palm raised upward or two fingers pointing up."
/>

<Cue
  title="Down"
  blurb="Lay down completely on the floor, and do not get up from down until released (ideally)."
  usefulFor="Settling in public spaces, calm resting at home, de-escalating arousal."
  howToTrain="From a sit, lure a treat straight down between their paws and slowly pull forward until elbows touch the ground; build duration before releasing."
  nonVerbal="Flat hand moving downward toward the floor."
/>

<Cue
  title="Stay"
  blurb="Dog remains in their current position (sit, down, or stand) until given an explicit release cue."
  usefulFor="Safety around open doors, waiting while preparing food, preventing bolting."
  howToTrain="Build 3Ds (Duration, Distance, Distraction) incrementally; ask for stay, pause briefly, mark and reward before releasing with 'Free'."
  nonVerbal="Stop-sign hand gesture (palm facing dog with fingers pointing up)."
/>

<Cue
  title="Leave It"
  blurb="Tells the dog to ignore or disengage from an object, food item, or scent on the ground."
  usefulFor="Safety against dropped food/medication, trash on walks, wildlife, or other dogs."
  howToTrain="Present treat in closed fist; wait until dog stops sniffing/licking the fist and turns away; mark and reward from the other hand."
  nonVerbal="Hand blocking or pointing away from item."
/>

<Cue
  title="Touch"
  blurb="Dog touches their nose directly to your open palm (different than 'shake' / paw)."
  usefulFor="Guiding movement without pulling, redirecting focus away from triggers, building confidence."
  howToTrain="Present flat open palm a few inches from dog's nose; when nose makes contact with skin, immediately mark and reward."
  nonVerbal="Open palm presented flat towards the dog."
/>

<Cue
  title="Heel"
  blurb="Walking politely right by handler's side without pulling or forging ahead."
  usefulFor="Navigating crowded sidewalks, passing other dogs, structured urban walks."
  howToTrain="Reward frequently at hip level while moving; change directions whenever tension appears on the leash."
  nonVerbal="Tapping left thigh / hip."
/>
</Card>

## Intermediate

These are things that I train with my regular clients once we have the basics down that I find helpful while sitting.

<Cue
  title="Up"
  blurb="Distinguishes between stay time and go time; prompts the dog to stand up on all four paws from a sit or down position."
  usefulFor="Resetting position before beginning another trick, game, or walk transition."
  howToTrain="From a sit or down, lure treat forward at nose level so dog steps up onto all fours; mark when standing."
  nonVerbal="Upward hand scoop gesture."
/>

<Cue
  title="Stop"
  blurb="Specifically stop while running or moving. Doesn't have to be a stay or a sit — just stopping momentum."
  usefulFor="Used while on jogs, runs, or off-leash sprints; helpful for safety at intersections or unexpected obstacles."
  howToTrain="While jogging together, give cue 'Stop', pause forward momentum, and reward immediately when dog halts."
  nonVerbal="Raised arm / fist held high."
/>

<Cue
  title="Go"
  blurb="Cue after stay signalling it is okay to go after a specific item, toy, or food."
  usefulFor="Releasing from a wait to fetch a toy, grab food bowl, or run to a designated area."
  howToTrain="Place dog in stay, place toy or bowl a short distance away, return to dog, cue 'Go' with directional gesture."
  nonVerbal="Directional arm sweep or pointing toward the target."
/>

## Location Specific

Cues specific to home furniture or designated resting spots.

<Cue
  title="Couch"
  blurb="Prompts dog to jump onto the couch and settle."
  usefulFor="Invited cuddle time or designated couch resting spot."
  howToTrain="Tap couch cushion, cue 'Couch', lure dog up, and reward when resting in position."
  nonVerbal="Tapping couch cushion."
/>

<Cue
  title="Off"
  blurb="Specifically used for getting off of couch, bed, counters, etc. Intentionally separate from 'down'."
  usefulFor="Maintaining boundaries, personal space, visitor comfort, and keeping furniture clean."
  howToTrain="When dog is on furniture, say 'Off', lure feet toward floor, and reward when all paws are on the ground."
  nonVerbal="Sweeping downward gesture pointing away from furniture to floor."
/>

<Cue
  title="Place"
  blurb="Sends the dog to their dog bed or mat and asks for a settle."
  usefulFor="Doorbell rings, dinner time, keeping dog calm while guests enter the home."
  howToTrain="Lure to bed, cue 'Bed', reward with multiple treats once on the bed to build high value for remaining there."
  nonVerbal="Pointing directly at the bed."
/>

## Advanced

These "tricks" are fun and flashy, but also help serve an important role in keeping dogs mentally stimulated, physically active, and engaged.

<Cue
  title="Spin"
  blurb="Dog spins a complete 360-degree circle in a designated direction (spin left or spin right)."
  usefulFor="Mental stimulation, physical flexibility, shaking off tension or arousal."
  howToTrain="Lure dog with treat in a full circle around their body; differentiate directional cues ('Spin Left' vs. 'Spin Right')."
  nonVerbal="Circular hand rotation in desired direction."
/>

<Cue
  title="Roll Over"
  blurb="From a down position, dog rolls completely over onto their back and returns to down position."
  usefulFor="Body awareness, physical flexibility, fun trick engagement."
  howToTrain="From down, lure treat from nose towards shoulder and around behind neck to guide rollover motion."
  nonVerbal="Circular rolling motion with finger or hand."
>
