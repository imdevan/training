## ABCs

The foundational framework for understanding how dogs interpret signals, execute behaviors, and learn from outcomes.

- **Antecedent (Signal):** The trigger, prompt, cue, or environmental context that signals the opportunity for a behavior.
- **Behavior (Observable Response):** The physical, observable action performed by the dog in response to the antecedent.
- **Consequence (Result):** The outcome that immediately follows the behavior (generally something added to or removed from the environment).

### Types of Consequence

- **Reinforcer:** Any consequence or response that **increases** the future likelihood of the behavior occurring again.
- **Punisher:** Any consequence or response that **decreases** the future likelihood of the behavior occurring again.

## Four Quadrants of Operant Learning

The four quadrants of operant conditioning (adapted from Good Pup). The primary goal is always to stay within **Positive Reinforcement** as a humane, effective training philosophy. However, it is essential to understand that any environmental variable can act as a reinforcer or punisher, and human reactions can sometimes unintentionally reinforce or punish behaviors.

#### Positive Reinforcement (+R)

The addition of something pleasant to the environment that influences the likelihood a behavior will increase; it strengthens behavior (e.g. giving a high-value treat for a successful sit).

#### Negative Reinforcement (-R)

The subtraction of something unpleasant from the environment that influences the likelihood a behavior will increase; it strengthens behavior (e.g. releasing gentle leash pressure the moment the dog turns toward you).

#### Negative Punishment (-P)

The subtraction of something pleasant from the environment that influences the likelihood a behavior will decrease; it weakens behavior (e.g. turning your back and withholding attention when a dog jumps up to greet).

#### Positive Punishment (+P)

The addition of something unpleasant to the environment that influences the likelihood a behavior will decrease; it weakens behavior (e.g. adding a physical or verbal reprimand — avoided in positive, force-free training).

</Card>

## Extinction / Extinguish

The systematic withholding of reinforcement for a previously reinforced behavior, resulting in the behavior gradually decreasing until it is extinguished or disappears entirely.

:::tip[Extinction Burst]
When reinforcement is first withheld, the behavior may temporarily increase in intensity (an extinction burst) before fading away. Consistency is critical.
:::

</Card>

## 2 Learning Modes

Dogs process and adapt to their world through two primary learning modes:

### Associative Learning

Rewarding or pairing outcomes with behaviors the dog is naturally performing to build positive, involuntary associations (Classical Conditioning).

- **Examples:**
  - _Potty Training:_ Dog goes potty outside, dog gets praise and relief of potty break, over time learns this is where to go potty.
  - _Loose-Leash Attention:_ Dog walks beside you without asking, give a treat, over time learns walking next to you = good things.

### Operant Learning

Actively asking for a specific, voluntary behavior and rewarding successful choices to shape future actions.

- **Example:**
  - "Do a trick, get a treat" (e.g. asking for a "Sit", dog chooses to sit, dog earns a reward).
