## Puppy Pushups

Chaining sit + laydown in random intervals, followed by a treat reward.

#### Teaches Difference Between Sit and Down

Helps build rapid physical responsiveness and clear mental distinction between the two positions.

#### Stimulates and Engages in Small Spaces

Provides quick mental and physical engagement without requiring much room.

#### Redirection Tool

Can also be used as an effective de-escalating redirection tool when excitement starts to ramp up.

## Fetch

A high-energy game most dogs already love doing, elevated with structured cues and impulse control.

#### Integrate Cues

Add cues for "come" on ball retrieval, and "drop it" on drop off.

#### High-Energy Disengagement

Training in this space can be really good for helping train dogs to disengage during high energy moments.

#### Confidence Around Distractions

Playing small fetching nearby other dogs can build confidence and also help with reactiveness.

:::note[Gotchas & Best Practices]
#### Preserve the Integrity of "Down"

One thing I try not to do is go directly from a "down" command into chasing after the ball, in order to reinforce that "down" is always "down and stay down" until released.

#### Reset with "Up"

Personally, I will always use the "up" command if the dog is in sit or down, make sure his butt is off-ground, sometimes add another trick, and then throw the ball.
:::

## Look at the Dog, Look at Me

A proactive focus and counter-conditioning game practiced from a sub-threshold distance where the dog starts noticing a dog.

Optionally sitting or in a down, positioned from a distance just where the dog is starting to react to or notice another dog.

### How to Play

1. **Acknowledge the trigger:** Point out the dog, making sure your dog notices.
2. **Redirect focus:** Give the "look at me" cue.
3. **Reward:** Immediately reward with high-value treats upon successful eye contact.

#### What It Teaches

Other dogs are okay to notice, other dogs are not a threat, and looking back at the guardian when seeing a dog yields high rewards.

#### Playable Locations

Sitting outside during neighborhood walks, or observing calmly from a balcony, porch, or window.

## Jumping on the Couch

Cycle between couch and off cues while systematically practicing moving further away from the couch.

### How to Play

1. **Initial setup:** Stand next to the couch.
2. **Cue couch:** Say "Couch".
3. **Reward on couch:** When the dog jumps and goes into down position on the couch, reward with a treat.
4. **Cue off:** Say "Off". When the dog jumps off, reward with a less valuable treat or half treat.
5. **Increase distance:** Take a step away from the couch and say "Couch".
   - Only say "couch" once and wait. If he doesn't go, then point.
   - If he still doesn't go:
     - That is okay! Note this position as the current **benchmark**.
     - Pause, do another trick, or otherwise re-engage.
     - Then go one step closer back toward the couch from the benchmark.
     - Repeat the loop until the dog successfully executes the cue from this position before moving back to the benchmark.
6. **Celebrate and reset:** When the dog executes the command correctly, give treat and praise. Say "Off", and reward with a half treat when he jumps down.
