<CollapsibleCard title="Zig Zag">

a.k.a. "The Sand Walk", a.k.a. "Never Let Them Know Your Next Move", a.k.a. "The Yoyo"

This is a game I like to throw in randomly on walks, especially with dogs that like to pull a lot and helps train loose leash walking.

You can play this game on any walk with no additional equipment required. In the park or on the side walk.

### How to play

1. Walk in any direction.
2. As soon as the dog beings to pull, change direction.
3. Repeat.

You will likely end up staying in the same location.

For additional encouragment you can give the dog a treat if they decide to walk beside you with a loose leash.

### Why its helpful

This game helps train the dog to listen rather than assume they know the direction.

<CollapsibleCard title="Fetch">

A high-energy game most dogs already love doing, elevated with structured cues and impulse control.

#### Integrate Cues

Add cues for "come" on ball retrieval, and "drop it" on drop off.

#### High-Energy Disengagement

Training in this space can be really good for helping train dogs to disengage during high energy moments.

#### Confidence Around Distractions

Playing small fetching nearby other dogs can build confidence and also help with reactiveness.

:::note[Gotchas & Best Practices]

#### Preserve the Integrity of "Down"

One thing I try not to do is go directly from a "down" command into chasing after the ball, in order to reinforce that "down" is always "down and stay down" until released.

#### Reset with "Up"

Personally, I will always use the "up" command if the dog is in sit or down, make sure his butt is off-ground, sometimes add another trick, and then throw the ball.
:::

</CollapsibleCard>

<CollapsibleCard title="Hide and Seek, Fetch">

  A variation on Fetch!

### How to play

  1. Use "Down" and "stay" cue to get dog to lay down facing away from where you plan to throw or otherwise "hide" the ball / toy.
  2. Use "go" cue to signal to dog to go and find the ball.
  3. Help dog find ball using only "yes" and "no" markers to inidicate if they are going the correct direction.

### Advanced

  Once you have played "yes" and "no" a few games. Try introducing left and right!

  While dog is searching:

  1. Provide direction "left"
    1.a If dog goes towards ball say "yes!"
    1.b If dog does go gowards ball say "no" (you can say it in a cute way)

### Why I like this game

- Helps reinforce "yes" and "no" markers.
- Helps dog with find skills and builds trust in their finding ability.
- Helps build trust in dogs finding abilities.
- Helps reinforce that "no" is not a BAD thing it is just a thing.

<CollapsibleCard title="Puppy Pushups">

Chaining sit + laydown in random intervals, followed by a treat reward.

#### Teaches Difference Between Sit and Down

Helps build rapid physical responsiveness and clear mental distinction between the two positions.

#### Stimulates and Engages in Small Spaces

Provides quick mental and physical engagement without requiring much room.

#### Redirection Tool

Can also be used as an effective de-escalating redirection tool when excitement starts to ramp up.

</CollapsibleCard>

<CollapsibleCard title="Look at the Dog, Look at Me">

A proactive focus and counter-conditioning game practiced from a sub-threshold distance where the dog starts noticing a dog.

Optionally sitting or in a down, positioned from a distance just where the dog is starting to react to or notice another dog.

### How to Play

1. Start from a safe space: dog should be pretty chill (not in range of be actively reacting to other dogs).
1. **Acknowledge the trigger:** Point out the dog, making sure your dog notices, and looks that direction.
1. **Redirect focus:** Give the "look at me" cue.
1. **Reward:** Immediately reward with high-value treats upon successful eye contact.

### Why I like this game

- Helps dog learn that it's okay to be aware other dogs.
- Reinforces desired behavior when dog is interacting with the world.
- Reinforces dog's agency and choice to be a cool-cucumber.

#### What It Teaches

Other dogs are okay to notice, other dogs are not a threat, and looking back at the guardian when seeing a dog yields high rewards.

#### Playable Locations

Sitting outside during neighborhood walks, or observing calmly from a balcony, porch, or window.

</CollapsibleCard>

<CollapsibleCard title="Jumping on the Couch">

Cycle between couch and off cues while systematically practicing moving further away from the couch.

### How to Play

1. **Initial setup:** Stand next to the couch.
2. **Cue couch:** Say "Couch".
3. **Reward on couch:** When the dog jumps and goes into down position on the couch, reward with a treat.
4. **Cue off:** Say "Off". When the dog jumps off, reward with a less valuable treat or half treat.
5. **Increase distance:** Take a step away from the couch and say "Couch".
   - Only say "couch" once and wait. If he doesn't go, then point.
   - If he still doesn't go:
     - That is okay! Note this position as the current **benchmark**.
     - Pause, do another trick, or otherwise re-engage.
     - Then go one step closer back toward the couch from the benchmark.
     - Repeat the loop until the dog successfully executes the cue from this position before moving back to the benchmark.
6. **Celebrate and reset:** When the dog executes the command correctly, give treat and praise. Say "Off", and reward with a half treat when he jumps down.

</CollapsibleCard>

<CollapsibleCard title="Hurry Up and Wait">

  It's like a "drinking game" but for sits!

### How to play

  1. First we set ground rules for when we want the dog to sit. These are the ones I use:

- Before going through a door.
- Randomly between fetches.
- At the end of every other song I am listening to on walk.

  1. Go on a walk!
  2. Whenever one of the things happen. Do a sit! :D

### Why I like this game

- Helps reinforce pateience. For everyone involved :D
- Helps dog build self control
- Reinforces the "sit" cue

### Fun additional rules

  Swap the "sit" cue for other cues, or puppy pushups!
